      How the prototype maps to the flowchart
      1. Input: every 30 simulated minutes, each of the 16 smart bins reports its fill level. Citizen complaints, truck GPS positions and the collection history are also inputs.
      2. Send and validate: the central system rejects impossible readings (outside 0–100%) and sudden drops with no logged collection. When a reading is rejected, the bin keeps its last valid value. Duplicate complaints for the same bin are merged.
      3. Decision: a bin needs pickup when it reaches the threshold or has an open complaint. Trucks are dispatched right away for complaints, Critical bins or three or more pending bins. A single non-urgent bin waits up to 60 minutes so nearby bins can be batched into one route. If no bin needs pickup, the system continues monitoring.
      4. Priority: score = fill % + 25 for a complaint + 15 if overflowing + 10 for sensitive sites (market, hospital, school, transit) + 0.5 per hour since the last pickup (capped at 24 h). A score of 110 or more is Critical.
      5. Optimised route: bins are assigned to available trucks within capacity (800 kg). Each route visits Critical bins first, then uses nearest-neighbour ordering improved by 2-opt along the street grid. The saving is measured against a fixed-order route.
      6. Notify and assign: the sanitation team receives the route and the driver is dispatched. Trucks marked for maintenance are skipped.
      7. Collect and record: the bin resets, the weight is logged and any linked complaint is resolved. The citizen is notified, and the authority as well if the bin is damaged.
      8. More pending? The truck goes to the next stop or picks up newly urgent bins. Otherwise it returns to the depot to unload, which ends the collection cycle while monitoring continues.

      In a real deployment, each bin would carry an ultrasonic fill sensor with a microcontroller (for example, an ESP32) reporting over GSM or LoRa. The trucks would report GPS, and complaints would come from a mobile app.
